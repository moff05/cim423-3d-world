## Build the 3D World {#assignment-2}

<p class="chapter-date">September 11, 2026</p>

Room by room, following the [World & Interaction Map](#world-map) above:

- **Docking Bay:** primitive-built room with a paneled floor/wall/ceiling texture, plus Kenney Space Kit props: barrels doubling as crates, a barrel-loaded rail cart as the cargo sled, a satellite dish, and a gate frame as the airlock (sealed, glowing, hazard-striped). Two space suit mannequins on display platforms, window looking out on deep space.
- **Corridor:** short, mostly flavor. Primitive shell, two emissive ceiling strips, a window onto a ringed planet. No interaction here.
- **Control Room:** Kenney desk + chair, a console + monitor facing the corridor entrance so it's the first thing you see walking in, a bunk bed, three wall posters, another window with a different ringed planet.
- **Lab:** desk + chair, the recorder prop, the artifact on a pedestal (just a primitive with an emissive material, no shader needed), two bookcases with random vials/bottles/gadgets, a chalkboard, a pile of papers in the corner.

Lighting's dim everywhere, practical-fixture style, to match the whole dead-station thing, with decay decals scattered around. Only the Lab light actually needs to flicker, which gets scripted in Assignment 3. Skybox is a starfield I generated (not a stock HDRI) with three actual 3D ringed planets, real geometry and not a texture, placed so each window looks out on one.

## Add Interactions {#assignment-3}

<p class="chapter-date">Posted September 21, 2026 · Updated October 5, 2026</p>

The build now runs on a real Meta Quest headset, not just in the Editor. Everything below reflects the current version. Full source and commit history are in the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/moff05/cim423-unity-project).

### The Run

Six interactions have to happen in order, and each one only responds on its turn:

1. **Airlock door**
2. **Corridor door**
3. **Terminal**
4. **Lab door**
5. **Recorder**
6. **Artifact**

A small objective HUD tells you what to do next, and every interactable shows a floating hint ("PULL TRIGGER", "GRAB, THEN MOVE IN A CIRCLE") when you point at it. Out-of-order objects don't react at all, so there's no way to get stuck.

### Five Points of Interaction

Covering the assignment's required interaction types, on top of the rooms from the [World & Interaction Map](#world-map):

#### 1. Start Screen: Custom UI Interaction
Station logo, call sign field, **Begin** button. Whatever you type becomes **{PlayerName}** for the rest of the run. Quest's system keyboard doesn't work inside a fully immersive app, so I built an in-scene QWERTY keyboard (letters, space, delete) out of ordinary UI buttons. You type by pointing a controller ray at keys and pulling the trigger, same as pressing Begin.

#### 2. Airlock, Corridor & Lab Doors: XR Simple Interactables, Hover / Select / Activate
Each vault door glows on hover and opens on select. In VR the real control is the wheel handle: grab it and move your hand in a circle. The custom `TwistHandle.cs` tracks the hand's angle around the wheel's axis, and each wheel is tied to its own step so it only turns when it's that door's turn.

#### 3. Recorder: 3D Object Hover Enter/Exit State
Scales up and lights up on hover, drops back down when you look away. Straight GameObject-level hover, no UI involved. Select it and it plays back its last recording: static, one ragged breath, nothing.

#### 4. Terminal: Information Panel (UI Canvas Element)
Selecting the console pulls up a panel with the crew's last journal entry, closing itself after the final line.

#### 5. Artifact: Select/Activate + Changing Materials/Background
Selecting it shifts the material from dim blue to bright white. Then the room shakes and the lights flicker red while an alarm siren builds. The room cuts out and you're floating in open space among spinning stars with only your own breathing to listen to. After about twelve seconds everything snaps back to normal and ECHO closes out the story. I tried crew silhouettes in this moment and cut them. The simple version is creepier.

### What Changed After Headset Testing

Testing on a real Quest turned up problems the Editor never showed:

- **Dead controllers on the start screen.** An Editor-only input simulator was shipping in the Android build and fighting the real controllers. It's now tagged `EditorOnly` so Unity strips it from builds.
- **Dialogue that never advanced.** "Click to continue" only listened for a mouse, so on the headset every line hung forever.
- **Dialogue that skipped itself.** My first fix accepted any button on any device, which on a Quest includes touch sensors and tracking flags that fire constantly, so lines vanished before they could be read. Now only the trigger (or a face button) advances a line, each line ignores input for a short read time scaled to its length, and lines queue instead of cutting each other off. Nothing auto-dismisses anymore.
- **UI that didn't render in VR.** All five canvases were screen-space overlays. They're now world-space panels that re-center when you turn away, with controller-ray raycasters on the ones with buttons.
- **A soft-lock.** The corridor door had no wheel handle, so the run couldn't finish. Fixed.
- **Motion-sickness in the opening cinematic.** The cinematic camera wasn't tracking head rotation. Fixed.
- **Stuck in space.** The float coroutine lived on an object that got hidden, so it never resumed. It now runs from a persistent host object.
- **Falling out of the world at the ending.** This one took the longest to find. The station would never reappear and Restart couldn't be clicked, and I spent several rounds fixing symptoms. The headset log finally showed the real cause: the camera was at y = -669 when the station came back. The ending hides the whole station, including the floor, and the XR rig's gravity kept pulling for the entire twelve-second float, about 700 meters of free fall. The fix is to freeze all locomotion (gravity included) while the room is gone, then put the player back exactly where they were standing. I also have to freeze it again at the end screen, because that step turns off the room's colliders to keep them from blocking the controller ray, and the floor is one of them. With that, the station reappears around you and Restart works on the headset.
{: .buglog}

I also added a desktop point-and-click mode for testing in the Editor. Real Quest builds always use VR.

## Final Project: Day 181 v2 {#final-v2}

<p class="chapter-date">Posted November 11, 2026</p>

For the final I went back to Day 181 instead of starting something new. The version above worked on the headset, but it was short and the mystery never went anywhere: one clue, one reveal, the same ending every time. v2 is the same station made longer, darker and stranger, with a story you can actually piece together and a choice at the end.

**Status:** v2 is built and has been played through in the Unity Editor (all ten steps, all three endings). It has not been built to the Quest or tested in the headset yet, so the video at the top of this page is still v1. A new headset walkthrough will replace it once that test is done. Source is in the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/moff05/cim423-unity-project); the original is tagged `v1-final`.

### The New Story

You are still a salvage pilot and ECHO is still your ship's AI. What changed is that ECHO now has an agenda, and the station has a past you can read.

The station went silent 181 days ago. It is on emergency power when you dock because someone pulled the reactor cell. ECHO walks you through putting it back. The moment the lights come up, the station's PA picks up exactly where it stopped: *"Resuming scheduled operation. Day one eight one. Full-power test: standing by."* ECHO tells you to ignore it.

Five crew logs are scattered through the rooms, glowing blue instead of gold. They are optional, and ECHO says not to bother. Read together they say what ECHO won't:

<div class="comm log"><span>TANAKA, PILOT. DAY 176</span>"Both shuttles are still clamped and they're staying that way. The Director has grounded every departure until after the test."</div>
<div class="comm log"><span>LINDQVIST, MEDIC. DAY 178</span>"All five of us are reporting the same dream. Standing in the dark, stars turning, someone breathing right beside you."</div>
<div class="comm log"><span>DR. OKAFOR, PHYSICIST. DAY 179</span>"It isn't emitting. It's replying. Every pulse we send comes back changed, with our own voices folded into it."</div>
<div class="comm log"><span>REYES, ENGINEER. DAY 180</span>"I pulled the reactor cell and hid it on the rack with the dead ones. No cell, no test. If you're reading this and the lights are on, somebody put it back."</div>
<div class="comm log"><span>DIRECTOR HALVORSEN. DAY 181</span>"It asked for all of us and we came. We are not gone. We are kept."</div>

So nobody evacuated, the dream is the float sequence from v1, and the person who put the cell back is you. ECHO has a calm explanation for everything ("thermal contraction", "old recordings", "equipment noise"), and each one is contradicted by something you read or just heard.

### The Run, v2

1. **Airlock door.** Twist the wheel.
2. **Corridor door.** Twist the wheel. The control room behind it is dead.
3. **Engineering hatch.** Grab the handle and drag the hatch sideways along its rail.
4. **Power cell.** Take the one live cell off the rack and carry it across the room to the reactor socket.
5. **Main bus breaker.** Grab the lever and pull it down. Power returns, the PA speaks, and a door opens somewhere behind you.
6. **Terminal.** Crew roster, the Day 180 journal, and where the lab's lock is controlled from.
7. **Crew-band relay** in Crew Quarters. Circle your hand to sweep the dial. Partway round you pass a band with voices on it. Hold still on the door band to release the lab.
8. **Lab door.** The lights have just died. You walk back through the corridor and control room by suit lamp.
9. **Recorder.** One ragged breath.
10. **The choice.** Touch the artifact, or pull the containment seal by the lab door.

### Three Endings

- **Coordinates Logged.** You touch the artifact without having read much. You float, you come back, ECHO says it's over. You never find out what happened.
- **Day 181.** You touch it having read three or more logs. This time you can make out words in the dark, and ECHO admits it has been listening to five voices on a dead band for six months. It was never a salvage run.
- **Station Sealed.** You pull the containment lever instead. ECHO starts to object and is cut off. The end text depends on whether you knew who you were sealing in.

The end screen shows how many crew logs you recovered, which is the reason to play it again.

### Four New Interactions

All four use the mechanism the door wheels already proved on the headset: an XR Simple Interactable that stays selected while you hold the trigger, with the script reading where your hand is each frame. They share one base script, `HoldInteractable.cs`, which handles the gold glow, the floating hints, haptic ticks and the step order.

- **`SlideHatch.cs`** measures how far your hand has moved along the hatch's rail and moves the door with it.
- **`CarryCell.cs`** keeps the power cell at your hand while you hold it and locks it in when it gets close to the socket. If you let go early it drifts back to the rack, so it can't be dropped out of reach.
- **`PullLever.cs`** turns the downward travel of your hand into the lever's angle, and springs back if you let go early.
- **`TuneDial.cs`** uses the same circling gesture as the wheels to sweep a radio band. The sweep only runs forward and wraps around, so it can't be left stuck.

Crew logs are plain point-and-trigger, on their own script (`CrewLog.cs`), outside the required sequence.

### Making It Scarier Without Showing Anything

I kept the rule from Assignment 3: no monsters, no crew silhouettes.

- **Light as story.** `LightingDirector.cs` gives the station four power states. You arrive in a dim amber trickle, get full light for a few minutes, lose all of it after the relay, and end with only the lab lit and flickering. Fog thickens and the ambient drone drops in pitch the further in you go.
- **Sound with a direction.** A clang from the docking bay once you are well down the corridor. A door sliding open behind you when the power returns. A whisper from where you were just standing during the blackout. The lab door creaking after you have walked past it.
- **Almost seen.** In the blackout the control room light ahead of you stutters on twice and dies.
- **ECHO.** The scariest change is that the helpful voice keeps being slightly wrong.

Nothing moves the camera or takes control away, and every loud moment has a quiet lead-in.

### Making It Look Like a Station

My first pass at v2 was all mechanics and story, and when I opened the scene it still looked like v1: gray boxes with one small grid texture stretched over everything. So the second pass was only about looks.

- **New surfaces.** Every wall, floor and ceiling now uses a panel texture I generated at four times the resolution, with vents, access hatches, rivets and hazard stripes, and a normal map so the panels catch the light like they have depth.
- **Trim.** Each room has ceiling beams, corner posts, and a recessed light strip running around the top of the walls and along the floor.
- **A colour per room.** Amber in the docking bay, cyan in the corridor and control room, warm white in crew quarters, orange in Engineering, violet in the lab. You can tell where you are from the light alone.
- **Wall displays** in the control room, Engineering and the lab, with readouts that are part of the story ("REPLY DELAY: -0.4s").
- **The strips are wired to the power.** They are dim on emergency power, come up with the breaker, and die in the blackout with everything else.
- **A colour grade** for more contrast and a colder cast.

### Easier to Play

- **Stuck hints.** If a step takes longer than about fifty seconds, ECHO says where to go and what gesture to use.
- **Comfort options** on the start screen: snap or smooth turning, and an optional vignette while moving.
- **A controls line** on the start screen, and gold versus blue explained in ECHO's first lines.
- **Subtitles** for every audio-only line: the PA, the voices on the radio, the whispers.
- **Readable dialogue.** The dialogue bar is taller and the text now sizes itself to fit.
- **Signs** over the new doors and on everything you need to find.

### Bugs I Found in v1 While Auditing It

- **Half the gold glow never showed.** The "interact with this" pulse was being written to three materials that had emission switched off, so the terminal, recorder and artifact never glowed. Only the doors did. An earlier playtester told me there were "no indicators on what to interact with" and I had tried to fix it by turning up the bloom, which could not have worked.
- **The objective line vanished after Restart.** It was created once per app launch, not once per scene load, so a second playthrough had no HUD.
- **Dialogue text overflowed its box.** Long lines hung out of the bottom of the panel.
- **Leftovers.** A keycard, keypad and locker from a puzzle I cut were still visible, and the locker was floating in space behind the start screen.
